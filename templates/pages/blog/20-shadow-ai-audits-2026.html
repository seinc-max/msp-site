No written AI policy

Law firms, accounting practices and financial advisory businesses often have staff using public AI tools with client data. No policy. No visibility. No documentation.

In May 2026, Canada’s Privacy Commissioner found that OpenAI’s initial training of ChatGPT did not comply with PIPEDA (PIPEDA Findings #2026-002). Law societies have warned that inputting confidential client information into generative AI tools can create serious confidentiality risks.

78% of people who use AI at work bring their own AI tools, and 80% at small and medium-sized companies (Microsoft Work Trend Index, 2024).

Why this happens

It's not incompetence. It's the math of small business.

Hiring a full-time IT person costs $65,000–$85,000 per year in salary. With benefits, equipment and training, the all-in cost is $83,000–$115,000+ (Unified Technology, 2026). A business with 20 people can't justify that expense.

So they ban ChatGPT. A memo goes out. The problem looks fixed on paper. Then employees just use their personal phones to summarize confidential documents.

That's the reactive trap.

What proactive IT actually does

A written policy is meant to do a few practical things:

  1. A documented AI policy gives you something to share. It is written so you can share it with your insurer and clients. We help you document and reduce privacy risk. We don't provide legal advice.
  2. An inventory of the tools in use. You can see which AI tools the team relies on, which are approved, and which should be shut down.
  3. Copilot permissions locked to what each person may see. AI can be used on the files that person is already allowed to open.
  4. Sensitive files encrypted at rest. Customer records, financial docs, and payroll stay protected if a laptop is lost.
  5. A written incident response plan. Everyone knows what to do if data is exposed.

The PIPEDA angle (for regulated industries)

PIPEDA is Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. It applies to most businesses that collect personal information in commercial activity. We help you document and reduce privacy risk. We don't provide legal advice.
