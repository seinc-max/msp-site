  1. Law firms, the night before a filing. The client asks whether the filing went in. The agent reads the matter note you approved. It does not draft the pleading, and it does not touch privileged instructions.
  2. Accounting practices, the week of the close. The client asks whether the return was filed. The agent reads the status you published. It does not invent a figure.
  3. Your bookkeepers, the morning the package is due. The client asks for last month's package. The agent points at the file you already shared.
  4. Your tax preparers, on a deadline night. The client asks which document is still missing. The agent lists the items you marked missing. It does not reply to the tax notice.
  5. Your financial advisors, the day before a review. The client asks when the meeting is. The agent reads the calendar note you approved.
  6. Your wealth managers, before a family meeting. The family asks for the agenda. The agent sends the agenda you wrote. It does not summarize the trust.
  7. Insurance agencies, on a renewal. The client asks whether the renewal was submitted. The agent reads the status on the file you updated.
  8. Your mortgage brokers, while the file is in underwriting. The client asks which documents are still outstanding. The agent lists them from your checklist.
  9. Your consultants, the week of a workshop. The client asks for the next date. The agent reads the date you posted.
  10. Your architecture and engineering firms, the day a set goes out. The client asks whether the drawings were sent. The agent reads the transmittal you logged. It does not stamp a drawing.
  11. Your HR consultancies, with a policy still in draft. The client asks for the status. The agent reports the status you set. It does not edit the draft policy.
  12. Your property managers, after a work order. The owner asks whether the ticket was opened. The agent reads the ticket you created.
